PhysGeo

Grounding Video Generation in Explicit Physical Simulation

Anonymous submission

A single photograph is turned into a physical scene, the scene is simulated, and a video model renders the simulated motion back into the photograph. What moves, where it goes and how it collides is decided by the simulator — the video model only has to make it look real.

Overview

A video can look convincing while depicting the wrong physical outcome. The problem is sharpest when several objects interact: the generator has to decide which body moves, how contact changes its motion and how that change spreads through the scene. Text prompts specify none of this precisely.

PhysGeo makes the outcome explicit. From the reference image and a few scene priors we build a rigid-body scene and simulate it. The resulting trajectory is projected into the photograph's camera as per-frame depth and surface normals, and a Cosmos video generator, adapted with a light-weight geometry interface, renders a video that follows this geometry while keeping the look of the photograph. The interface is learned from real robot videos with estimated depth, so no photorealistic simulator rendering is ever needed for training. Because the physics lives in the simulator, changing a mass or a contact changes the generated video, with the same image and the same prompt.

Method

1 Photo to physical scene

Objects are represented by reconstructed or fitted primitive meshes, and the camera is fitted to image landmarks so that simulated surfaces land on the right pixels. Robot motion is scripted; everything that is pushed, lifted, hit or dropped is simulated, with contact and penetration checks kept alongside the trajectory.

2 Geometry tokens

Depth and normals pass through the same video VAE as the frames. Their tokens share spatial positions with the video tokens and are interleaved frame by frame, so attention can tie an image patch to the surface behind it. The first frame stays clean and is restored after every solver step; LoRA adapts an otherwise frozen backbone.

3 Learned from real video

Training uses real manipulation clips with depth estimated by a monocular model and normals derived from it. Noise mixing and geometric augmentation bridge the gap between estimated depth at training time and clean simulator depth at test time.

Changing the physics, not the prompt

We change one physical parameter in the simulator and render again with the same photograph, prompt and seed. A text-only model can only be told about the change in words; here Wan2.2-5B receives the intervention in its prompt.
